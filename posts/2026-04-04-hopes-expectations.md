I hope to learn more about the local culture and how it shapes the care patients receive here. Even in these first few days, it’s clear that healthcare is closely tied to everyday life. We visited Ekwendeni Mission Hospital for a tour and got a glimpse of what is ahead. Seeing the range of care provided, and how much is done with limited resources was incredible. I expect we will encounter conditions like malaria, tuberculosis, and HIV more frequently, and I am interested to see how providers approach care when resources and access are not guaranteed. 

I also hope to better understand the role of community in healthcare and how relationships between providers and patients influence outcomes. There seems to be a strong connection that extends beyond the hospital, and I’m curious to see how that shapes trust and treatment. Personally, I think this experience will challenge me in a good way, especially in thinking about what really matters in medicine when you don’t have every resource available. I’m looking forward to learning as much as I can and gaining a different perspective that I can carry with me moving forward.

Ellie Goldman
Third-Year Medical Student
From Charleston, West Virginia
