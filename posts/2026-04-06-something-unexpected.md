Something that I didn’t realize before coming to Malawi is that their economy is experiencing a protracted time of crisis. We learned from our driver, Paul, that there has been extremely high rates of inflation. As one of the world’s poorest countries, this has resulted in many devastating outcomes within their communities. Families struggle to make ends meet as prices of food, fuel, and energy have increased and job opportunities have become more limited. School systems have also suffered. Many sporting and extracurricular activities for children have been cut due to lack of funding. Paul stated that the Kwacha, which is Malawi’s currency, has devalued by nearly 50%. According to BBC news, the Kwacha has depreciated against the US dollar by around 40-44% since 2020.

This has affected their healthcare systems by lower funding from their government and increasing costs to patients, who already have to take into consideration traveling costs as well. While touring Ekwendeni Mission Hospital, we learned that a lot of their equipment was given to them through donations. Dr. Glandson mentioned that they run out of supplies frequently, even to the extent of not having sutures available for patients in need. 

With these issues in mind, it is truly inspiring how grateful Malawians are for what they do have and how optimistic and hardworking they remain despite their trials. I have yet to hear one person complain. 

Jessica Adkins
