So far, in the beautiful country of Malawi, we have felt nothing but welcomed. It has truly been wonderful.

While I was incredibly excited to begin working in the hospital and clinics, I would be lying if I said I wasn’t also a bit nervous, even as a fourth-year medical student. The people here have been so kind and generous to us that I found myself hoping we could, in some small way, reflect the same level of care and compassion they immediately showed us.

Our morning began with a few small hiccups, as our walk to the hospital turned out to be a bit longer than expected. Still, from the moment we arrived, we were welcomed straight into the morning handoff to begin our day. We were introduced to the entire staff, and each person made a genuine effort to learn our names, something that did not go unnoticed!

I was placed in radiology today, led by Dr. Gomez, one of the kindest individuals I have had the pleasure of meeting. He has a true passion for teaching and was eager to share his knowledge with us. He consistently included us in patient care, and even the patients themselves made us feel welcome, often going out of their way to ensure we could observe their ultrasounds and X-rays. Some patients even shared words and tips from their language to help us communicate better. It was a powerful reminder of how much learning is built on the generosity of both teachers and patients alike.

After a meaningful morning in clinic, we were invited to a conference led by the hospital director and her staff, who once again welcomed us warmly. We had discussions about potential collaboration between our group and their hospital moving forward. Conversations that felt both inspiring and full of possibility!

However, My favorite part of the day came later, listening to my classmates and underclassmen share their own experiences. From pediatric surgery to maternity wards to inpatient care, everyone had a story filled with excitement and gratitude. The energy was contagious, and the conversations carried well into the evening!

Everyone walked away from their first day not only grateful, but genuinely excited for what tomorrow will bring!

Alec Phelps
Fourth-Year Medical Student
Future Anesthesiologist
From Milton, West Virginia
