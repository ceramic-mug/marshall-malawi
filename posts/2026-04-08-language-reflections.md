“Tawonga chomene” (Chitumbuka for “thank you very much”) has been the phrase of our trip thus far, and rightfully so. Though English and Chichewa are Malawi’s national languages, we have been learning Chitumbuka, the language commonly spoken in the northern regions where we are staying- a language as beautiful as its people. 

Chitumbuka is a Bantu language spoken in Malawi, Zambia, and Tanzania. Only about 2-7 million people speak it, so I haven’t found as many online resources for reading. Our best resource has definitely been learning from native speakers. My favorite memories include singing the hymns from Easter Sunday as well as the morning walks to Ekwendeni Mission Hospital when the kids run out to greet us with “Mwawuka uli?” (“How did you wake?”) I enjoy the bright smiles on everyone’s faces when they hear us practicing, and I feel very encouraged to keep trying even when I pronounce the new words incorrectly. 

“Feel welcome” is mentioned by every person we meet, and I feel it in every interaction I’ve had. In a meeting we had at the hospital, the executive director of the office of health  mentioned how much they love visitors, because it meant they have made their home welcoming to others. We are very lucky to have such amazing hosts who teach us all things Malawi, including new words and phrases we pick up each day. Everyone is excited to help us practice and teach us more about our temporary home. We are so grateful for the connections we have made and the kindness shown to us!

Even today, when we brought school supplies and toys to the local primary school, the children were teaching us new words about the area around us. My favorite new word was ‘nkhuku” (“chicken”) because there were free range chickens in the corn field nearby which made the kids giggle as we tried to repeat it back. Our friend Webster even drew words into the sand so that I could practice saying “Khalani makola” (“goodbye/stay well”) to them before we left. They were such a joy to be around! 

Every aspect of our journey thus far assures me that our travels and the work it took to make this trip happen are worth every moment. I am very eager to continue learning more words and sharing my new language with family and friends back home. For now, I hope everyone enjoys reading more about our travels and learning new words along the way.  Tiwonanenge! (“We’ll be seeing each other!”)

Isabella Griffiths
Third-Year Medical Student
From Ironton, Ohio
