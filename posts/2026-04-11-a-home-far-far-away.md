Despite being separated by one US state, several African nations, and the Atlantic ocean, for a total distance spanning more than 7,000 miles, I still am able to find strong similarities between our home and theirs. Surprisingly, even with remarkably different cultures, these revolve around basic social constructs and a group identity that persists stronger through their perceived challenges. 

Since arriving, we have been greeted warmly by locals in the village during our walks to and from the hospital. With talking to a few people, it is clear that, like Appalachia, this is not only a tightly knit population, but one that has a community identity tied to their region and history. They have great pride in their home, and instead of wishing to go somewhere new, they are much more concerned with improving and supporting their own community. 

Like our home, Ekwendeni also faces major socioeconomic challenges, shortages in healthcare, a lower average income than elsewhere in the country, and a stigma from other regions of the country about it being a poorer region (northern Malawi generally struggles more than its southern counterpart). In Huntington, we have similar problems, especially with being viewed by the rest of the country as “poorer / less educated”. Despite this, both regions remain having great pride in where they call home, and a resilient identity. Together, both of us continue to strive for a better future for our homes and our neighbors.

Thomas Mosman
Fourth-Year Medical Student
Future Family Medicine Physician
From Wheeling, West Virginia
