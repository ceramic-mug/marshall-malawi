Before the start of our journey, group dynamics were something we had all likely thought about. "I'm so glad so-and-so is coming." "I heard she's really nice." "I don't know them very well." All things I'd imagine were said. 

Interpersonal dynamics can be tenuous even under ideal circumstances. People step on others' toes inadvertently or simple remarks may be received differently when the speaker's joking tone goes unrecognized. As a self-described "glue guy", I was interested in how things could unfold. Would we grow closer over time, or crumble? 

Immediately, our group was thrust under less-than-ideal circumstances through ticket uncertainty looming over a long travel day. From my own perspective, there was concern over how this may impact affected individuals. Moreover, how would this effect the group? Would the stress spread like wildfire, or could it be managed and contained? Questions about the group's makeup were quickly quelled as I watched, not just leaders, but everyone reassured and encouraged those dealing with issues. Members were quick to lend a helping hand and look out for one another. A common thread that appeared over our whole trip. 

In Malawi, exposure to each other came swiftly. Partly due to our mode of transportation: two large Toyota Land Cruisers that required us to pile in together. Another crucial part was the set up at Soil's. While we had separate rooms, the dining room had one large, feast-style table. Large discussions, nightly "props", and jokes quickly bonded us to each other. Lastly, I think walking the 2.1 miles to the hospital everyday allowed for a wealth of various interactions, including practicing our Tumbuka, and getting to learn the flavors of everyone's personalities. 

Even with the most well-planned trip, bumps arise and must be dealt with. Complaints are inevitable, and a plan that makes everyone happy is wishful thinking. When issues arose, members were quick to listen, discuss, and help however they could. Making sure everyone's needs were met or sharing of supplies were common themes over our three weeks in Malawi. 

Another point of consideration was time. Again, would it be our friend or foe? From previous trips, I was fearful it would be a foe; however, to my and other's surprise, we haven't been at each other's throats and passive-aggressiveness isn't prevalent. Throughout our time together, reflection and appreciation were common themes within our group. 

In conclusion, our time in Malawi as a group has been more about celebrating each personality rather than a growing annoyance. And, while I often wear rose-tinted-glasses, I believe this holds true for others. 

Steven Alex Pribanich
